Background

My research focuses on developing trustworthy, explainable, and human-centered AI for healthcare. I work across clinical text, structured/tabular data, biomedical signals, and medical images, with an emphasis on building AI systems that are accurate, interpretable, fair, and clinically meaningful.

Current PhD Research

  • Clinical Natural Language Processing (Clinical NLP): Developing and evaluating NLP and large language model (LLM) methods for clinical and healthcare applications.
  • AI with Real-World Clinical Data: Working with patient data from the University of Pittsburgh Medical Center (UPMC) on AI-based clinical tasks, including clinical prediction, patient-level analysis, and decision support.
  • Trustworthy & Fair Clinical AI: Investigating bias, fairness, interpretability, and reliability of AI models, with a focus on identifying and mitigating disparities across patient populations.
  • LLMs in Healthcare: Exploring the use, evaluation, and reasoning capabilities of LLMs in clinical settings, including how well they capture clinically meaningful information and support reliable healthcare decisions.

Prior Research

  • Explainable AI for Healthcare: Developed interpretable ML models using demographic, behavioral, socioeconomic, family, and health information for disease and health-risk prediction.
  • Fairness & Bias Mitigation: Investigated racial and demographic bias in medical AI, particularly skin cancer prediction, and developed approaches to improve equitable model performance.
  • Biomedical Signal Analysis: Worked with EEG signals for emotion recognition and consumer preference prediction, using interpretability methods to identify important brain regions and electrodes.
  • Generative AI & Synthetic Data: Explored synthetic data generation and augmentation across text, tabular, and image modalities to address limited data and class imbalance.
  • LLMs for Structured Clinical Data: Investigated GPT-based approaches that transform tabular clinical data into text for disease prediction, minority-class augmentation, and interpretable clinical reasoning.
  • Federated & Efficient AI: Developed federated learning approaches combining neural feature extraction with boosting-based prediction for efficient distributed learning.

Across these areas, my goal is to develop clinically useful, transparent, equitable, and trustworthy AI systems that can support healthcare professionals and improve patient-centered outcomes.

Research Interests

My research focuses on trustworthy and human-centered AI for healthcare, with particular emphasis on Clinical Natural Language Processing (Clinical NLP), Large Language Models (LLMs), multimodal learning, and machine learning with real-world clinical data. My current PhD research involves developing, evaluating, and implementing AI methods for clinical prediction, analysis, and decision support using patient data from the University of Pittsburgh Medical Center (UPMC), with a focus on model interpretability, fairness, bias identification and mitigation, reliability, and clinically meaningful reasoning. More broadly, my methodological interests include explainable AI, generative modeling, neural networks, agentic AI and agentic modeling, synthetic data generation, and multimodal learning across clinical text, structured data, biomedical signals, and medical images.
